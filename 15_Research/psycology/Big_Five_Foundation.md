# Scientific Consensus

## 1. Big Five is the current scientific foundation for personality assessment.

Evidence:
- Consistently replicated across independent datasets.
- Stable across different populations.
- Strong empirical support.

Confidence: High

---

## 2. Personality should be represented hierarchically.

Hierarchy:

Big Five Domains

↓

Lower-Level Facets

↓

Individual Questionnaire Items

Confidence: High

---

## 3. Lower-level facets provide substantially more information than global personality domains alone.

The five domains are useful summaries.

Most clinically relevant information exists at the facet level.

Confidence: High

---

## 4. Not all facets contribute equally.

Some facets are much stronger indicators of their parent domain than others.

Future predictive systems should learn facet importance rather than assuming equal weights.

Confidence: Moderate–High

---

## 5. Short assessments can estimate Big Five with acceptable accuracy.

Evidence suggests that carefully selected questions can preserve much of the information contained in much longer questionnaires.

Confidence: High

---

## 6. Personality assessment should prioritize information quality rather than questionnaire length.

Adaptive assessment is likely to outperform fixed questionnaires.

Confidence: Moderate

---

# NIROS Decisions

Accepted:

- Big Five will become the first personality layer.
- Facets will become the second personality layer.
- Static questionnaires will not be the final user experience.
- Adaptive interviewing will become the long-term assessment strategy.

Deferred:

- Final facet selection.
- AI conversational estimation.
- Dynamic question selection.

Rejected:

- Using only five global personality scores.
- Long static personality questionnaires as the primary assessment method.
