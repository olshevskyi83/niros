# Main Hypothesis

## Primary hypothesis

A personalized acoustic-semantic protocol may modulate the therapeutic trajectory of a psychedelic session by shaping attention, emotional salience, bodily interpretation, autobiographical memory, and meaning-making.

## Secondary hypotheses

1. A digital profile can predict which language, symbols, voice styles, and narrative arcs are likely to feel safe or threatening.
2. Voice and prosody can affect perceived safety, surrender, resistance, and emotional processing.
3. Personalized scripts may reduce adverse psychological reactions compared with generic scripts.
4. In fibromyalgia, personalized protocols may target the relationship between pain, threat perception, body memory, and identity.

## Null hypothesis

Personalized acoustic-semantic protocols do not improve outcomes compared with standard supportive psychedelic therapy.
