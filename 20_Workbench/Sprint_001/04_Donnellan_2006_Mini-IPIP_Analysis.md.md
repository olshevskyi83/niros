# Sprint 001 — Paper Analysis

## Paper

**Donnellan, M. B., Oswald, F. L., Baird, B. M., & Lucas, R. E. (2006)**

**Title:** The Mini-IPIP Scales: Tiny-yet-effective measures of the Big Five Factors of Personality

**Status:** Foundation

---

# Objective

Determine whether a short personality inventory can accurately estimate the Big Five dimensions without requiring a long questionnaire.

---

# Evidence

## 1. Mini-IPIP preserves the Big Five structure.

The 20-item Mini-IPIP demonstrates psychometric properties similar to the original 50-item IPIP-FFM.

**Confidence:** High

---

## 2. Convergent validity is good.

Mini-IPIP scores correlate strongly with IPIP-FFM and IPIP-NEO across all five personality domains.

**Confidence:** High

---

## 3. Reliability is acceptable.

Although shorter than the original inventory, Mini-IPIP maintains good internal consistency and test-retest reliability.

**Confidence:** High

---

## 4. Criterion validity is preserved.

Mini-IPIP predicts affect, life satisfaction and other psychological outcomes in a manner similar to longer personality inventories.  [oai_citation:0‡2006-Donnellan-PA-MiniIPIP.pdf](sediment://file_000000000ffc71f59561be013ae8c75e)

**Confidence:** High

---

# NIROS Interpretation

- Long personality questionnaires are not always necessary.
- Carefully selected questions can estimate personality efficiently.
- Adaptive interviewing may outperform fixed short questionnaires by asking only the most informative questions.

---

# Limitations

- Measures only the five broad personality domains.
- Does not assess lower-level facets.
- Does not include clinical or psychedelic-specific constructs.

---

# Working Hypotheses

### H001

NIROS should minimise the number of explicit questionnaire items.

**Status:** Tentative

---

### H002

Adaptive questioning should replace fixed questionnaires whenever possible.

**Status:** Tentative

---

### H003

Question quality is more important than questionnaire length.

**Status:** Tentative
