# Sprint 001 — Paper Analysis

## Paper

**Goldberg, L. R. (1999)**

**Title:** A Broad-Bandwidth, Public-Domain Personality Inventory Measuring the Lower-Level Facets of Several Five-Factor Models

**Status:** Foundation

---

# Objective

Evaluate whether an open psychometric framework can serve as the personality foundation for NIROS.

---

# Evidence

## 1. Open psychometrics

Scientific progress benefits from public-domain personality inventories rather than proprietary questionnaires.

**Confidence:** High

---

## 2. Hierarchical personality structure

Big Five domains consist of many lower-level facets.

**Confidence:** High

---

## 3. Cross-inventory compatibility

IPIP demonstrates strong correspondence with major commercial personality inventories.

**Confidence:** High

---

## 4. Broad-bandwidth assessment

Lower-level facets provide richer personality information than only the five global domains.

**Confidence:** High

---

## 5. Open scientific collaboration

Psychometric tools should evolve through transparent scientific collaboration.

**Confidence:** High

---

# NIROS Interpretation

- NIROS should use an open psychometric philosophy.
- Personality assessment should contain multiple levels.
- IPIP is currently the strongest candidate for the personality foundation of NIROS.
- Proprietary questionnaires should be avoided whenever high-quality open alternatives exist.

---

# Limitations

- No clinical validation.
- No psychedelic outcome prediction.
- No AI-based interviewing.

---

# Working Hypotheses

### H001

NIROS should adopt an open psychometric framework.

**Status:** Tentative

---

### H002

Personality assessment should contain:

- Big Five domains
- Lower-level facets

**Status:** Tentative

---

### H003

Relevant facets should be estimated adaptively rather than measured exhaustively.

**Status:** Tentative
