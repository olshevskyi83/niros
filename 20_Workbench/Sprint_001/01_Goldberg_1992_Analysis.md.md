# Sprint 001 — Paper Analysis

## Paper

**Goldberg, L. R. (1992)**

**Title:** The Development of Markers for the Big-Five Factor Structure

**Status:** Foundation

---

# Objective

Determine whether the Big Five model should become one of the scientific foundations of NIROS.

---

# Evidence

## 1. Stable five-factor structure

The same five-factor personality structure consistently appeared across different datasets, participant groups and measurement methods.

**Confidence:** High

---

## 2. Representative markers

A carefully selected subset of personality adjectives can accurately estimate the underlying personality dimensions.

**Confidence:** High

---

## 3. Lexical hypothesis

Important personality characteristics become encoded in natural language and therefore can be measured through carefully selected descriptors.

**Confidence:** High

---

## 4. Reliability

Properly selected marker sets preserve the five-factor structure with high reliability.

**Confidence:** High

---

# NIROS Interpretation

- Big Five is a robust high-level framework for describing personality.
- NIROS should estimate personality rather than ask hundreds of fixed questions.
- Conversation may become an additional psychometric data source.
- Big Five is only the first layer of personality assessment.

---

# Limitations

- The paper explains measurement, not personality mechanisms.
- No clinical validation.
- No psychedelic applications.

---

# Working Hypotheses

### H001

Big Five should become the first psychometric layer inside NIROS.

**Status:** Tentative

---

### H002

Adaptive interviewing is preferable to long static questionnaires.

**Status:** Tentative

---

### H003

Additional personality layers will be required beyond Big Five.

Examples:

- Attachment
- Suggestibility
- Absorption
- Trauma
- Emotion Regulation
- Personal Values

**Status:** Tentative

---
